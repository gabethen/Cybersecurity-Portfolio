# Week 03 Reflection

## 1. What was the most important networking concept you learned this week?

The most important networking concept I learned this week is that network activity alone does not prove malicious activity. A SOC analyst must investigate the context surrounding a connection, including the user, device, destination, timing, and data transferred before making a decision. Understanding networking helps analysts follow digital footprints and build accurate investigation timelines.

---

## 2. How has your understanding of network traffic changed since the beginning of the week?

My understanding of network traffic has changed significantly. This week opened my eyes to how network traffic works and how information travels through packets. While I still need more practice to fully understand every concept, I now have a solid foundation. I also find it interesting that nearly every action performed on a computer or device creates a digital footprint that can be analyzed during an investigation.

---

## 3. Why is it important for a SOC analyst to gather evidence before declaring an incident malicious?

It is important for a SOC analyst to gather evidence before declaring an incident malicious because not every unusual event is an attack. Analysts must collect as much information as possible, identify indicators, and determine whether the activity is normal or suspicious before reaching a conclusion. Making assumptions without evidence can lead to false positives and unnecessary investigations.

This week strengthened my understanding of networking fundamentals and how they apply to cybersecurity investigations.
